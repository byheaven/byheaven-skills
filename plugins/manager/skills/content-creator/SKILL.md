# Content Creator

Every writing task starts with a **writing brief** that the user approves, and the draft follows that brief. Style is chosen for each piece from its reader and purpose.

A **writing task** is text whose value lies in how a human reader receives it: product and interface copy, posts, articles, introductions, documents written to be read such as a Vision, principles, or a PRD, and messages sent on the user's behalf. Conversation replies, status updates, code, commit and PR text, instructions for agents (skills, rules, charters, directives), and documents that record facts for reference (decision records, product behavior documents, architecture, runbooks, reviews) are not writing tasks.

## 1. Writing brief

Show the user the brief before drafting or revising, and draft only after the user approves it. For the user's own posts, articles, and books, follow the next section instead of drafting. A Vision, other project documents, and product copy go through this brief and an agent draft even when the user signs them.

- **Author**: who is speaking, as a specific person or voice (a product team; a product's in-app companion; the founder in a Vision).
- **Reader**: one specific person in a specific moment, described by what they already know, what they care about, and what would convince them. A platform or a category ("players", "investors") is too broad.
- **Purpose**: what the piece should achieve, and what the reader takes away: the value (what they now know, can do, or can decide) and the feeling (how they feel about the subject and the author).
- **Style**: the style that best serves this reader and purpose (tone, register, rhythm, length, structure, how vivid or plain), with two or three concrete references that model it well: published works, writers, products, or pieces the user already approved, each with what to take from it.

Reuse what the project has settled, such as a product's brand voice, a document's agreed structure, or an approved brief for the same series, and name it in the brief. Done when the user approves the brief as written or as changed. One approved brief covers the task's drafts and revisions until its author, reader, or purpose changes.

## The user's own posts, articles, and books

The user writes their own personal posts, articles, and books. Assist their writing; produce no draft, passage, or full text meant for publication, in any language. The writing brief is worked out with the user as part of the help.

- **Before they write**: ask the questions that draw out their view: what they actually think, why, the strongest objection, who it is for. Record their answers verbatim, spoken or typed. Gather what they have already written on the topic (notes, journal entries, saved highlights) and the outside sources they need, grouped by theme.
- **Outline**: they write it. Point out claims without evidence, steps the argument skips, and the strongest counterargument.
- **While they write**: check facts, find examples and sources, and ask questions when they are stuck. Offer no sentences meant to be pasted in.
- **After they write**: read as their intended reader and report where that reader would get lost, doubt them, or lose interest, without rewriting. Check facts and platform limits. Fix typos and punctuation directly; flag everything else.
- **Another language**: they write that version too; review it the same way.
- **A book**: keep a workspace for it where the user keeps their notes: their thesis, the chapter map, their own material for each chapter, sources, and the terms and examples to keep consistent. Maintain it, give reader feedback chapter by chapter, and check for contradictions; every chapter is theirs.

## 2. Draft

- Write to the approved brief. Where another style rule differs, the brief decides.
- Conceive each language in that language from the shared argument; a version in a second language is written from the argument by a writer who has not read the first version.
- Platform limits: `references/platforms.md`. Vision and governance documents: `references/governance-docs.md`. Revision rounds: `references/anti-perfectionism.md`.

## Floor

These hold whatever the brief says:

- When a draft you write speaks for the user, use the user's own claims, positions, and examples; organize and phrase them, and add none.
- Ground external claims per `references/freshness.md`. In a draft you write, soften an unconfirmed claim to what is known, or cut it; in the user's own writing, report it to them with what you found.
- The title and opening promise what the body delivers. Present motives you infer as your reading of them.
- Product copy also holds the `experience-design` skill's red lines.

## Boundaries

Whether to publish stays with the user unless a standing authorization covers the specific path.
