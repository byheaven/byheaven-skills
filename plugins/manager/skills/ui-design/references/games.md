# Games and playful surfaces

Games go further than tools: the player learns by playing, and some discovery is part of the fun. The player's attention belongs to play, so the interface stays out of the way.

## Teaching

- In casual and short-session games, a player understands what to do within seconds without reading; deeper games may teach over a longer arc, still by play.
- Where the mechanics are simple, the first round is the tutorial: introduce one mechanic at a time in a safe space, let its consequence be obvious, then repeat and raise the difficulty.
- Show the rule in the world (a ghost hand, a highlighted target, a disabled control that lights up) before any sentence about it.
- A hint appears when the player is stuck for a while, once, at the spot where they are stuck.
- Tell only the rules a player cannot discover by playing; leave techniques and secrets for the player to find.
- Rules that live off screen, and the reason for a failure the player cannot see, are stated, in as few words as the moment allows.

## Feedback and reward

- Every input gets an immediate response; direct control responds within about 100 ms.
- Feedback is richer than in tools and proportional to the event: animation, sound, haptics, and numbers carry most of it; text is the last channel.
- A reward is shown as the thing itself moving into place (coins flying to the counter, "+20"), not as a sentence announcing it.
- Failure is gentle and fast to retry, and shows the one cause the player can act on.

## Interface

- Information lives in the world where it can (a sign, a character's line, an object's state) rather than in an overlay.
- The HUD carries only what the current moment needs; everything else waits for a pause.
- One consistent grammar for icon plus number across the game: the same icon always means the same resource.
- A companion or character may carry a rule as one spoken line in play instead of a printed paragraph, without inventing facts or advice the game does not support.

## Sharing

- Share copy sounds like the player speaking to a friend, carries the feeling of the moment, and matches what the friend will find; it never pressures or tricks the friend into tapping.
- At most one share prompt per round, never forced.
