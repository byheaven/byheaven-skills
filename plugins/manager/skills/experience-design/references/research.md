# Research and judgment

Pick the cheapest check that can change the decision. Report which kind of evidence each conclusion rests on: an agent's prediction, or an observation of real people.

## What an agent can run alone

These predict and rank problems; their findings are hypotheses until real people confirm them.

- **Cognitive walkthrough**, once per primary person: at each step, would they try to reach the right result, notice the control that does it, connect that control to their goal, and see that it worked? Best for new or unusual flows; standard patterns need less.
- **Heuristic review** against Nielsen's ten heuristics. Separate passes by independent reviewers find more than one long pass.
- **Overhead count**: steps, decisions, waits, re-entries, and requests before first value.
- **Trunk test** on deep screens: where am I, what is this, what can I do, how do I leave.
- **Red-lines audit** against the red-lines reference.
- **Automatable accessibility checks**: contrast, target size, labels and accessible names, focus order, keyboard reachability.
- **Funnel reading**, when the product is instrumented: where people drop out, not why.

## What needs real people

- **Small usability rounds**: three to five people from one user group, watching them try the real job; fix the worst problems with the smallest change, then run another round. Several small rounds beat one large one.
- **Five-second test** for an entry screen: after a short look, can they say what it is and what to do? The duration is a convention, not a law.
- **First-click test** for navigation and labels: a right first click strongly predicts success.
- **Tree test** of a structure without visual design, and **card sorting** (about 15 people) to discover how people group things.
- **Diary study** for habits, return, and motivation over days or weeks.

## When to ask for real people

Ask when the decision rests on first impressions, comprehension, feelings, whether people come back, or whether they regret a choice. Name the smallest test that would settle it and who could run it; an untested reaction stays reported as unknown.
