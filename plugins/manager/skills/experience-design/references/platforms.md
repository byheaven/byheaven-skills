# Platforms

Platform rules change; confirm a platform's current official guidance before relying on a specific limit below.

## WeChat mini programs and mini games

- The default session is a quick visit: people arrive, finish, and leave. Design for that, and earn any return with value.
- Every entry (a share card, a QR code, search, a service notification) is a cold start. Open at the exact state the entry promises and orient the person there.
- There is no install step, so give value before login or authorization; request each permission at the moment it is needed, with the reason.
- Subscription messages need the person's consent: one-time messages need it for each use, and long-term messages exist only for qualifying service categories. Design notifications around that consent.
- Rewards for sharing, share-to-unlock, recruiting friends' help or votes, and required follows break platform rules as well as the red lines.
- Leave the system capsule area at the top right clear, and keep the page stack shallow.
- Minors' play-time and spending limits apply to games in China.

## Native apps

- Follow the platform's navigation patterns, such as the tab bar and the back gesture.
- Restore the person's state after the system ends the app.
- Some system permission prompts, such as notifications or the camera on iOS, show only once; after a refusal the person must change it in settings. Explain the value just before triggering the prompt, at the moment of need.
- Notifications require the person's authorization on iOS, either explicit or provisional (delivered quietly until the person decides); earn them.
- Offline and weak-network states are part of the journey.

## Web

- The URL is state: a link or a refresh returns the person to the same place, and the back button works and is never hijacked.
- People arrive mid-journey from search and links.
- Keyboard and screen-reader paths work end to end.
- Consent banners offer a reject choice as prominent as accept.

## Internal tools

- Sessions are long and repeated: density, keyboard shortcuts, and bulk actions matter, and overhead costs more because it repeats.
- The primary person is the frequent user; new staff are served without slowing the frequent user down.
