# Plans

## Before a plan settles

A product or technical plan whose choice is consequential or costly to reverse settles only after two steps, and a plan costly to reverse after a third. Lay out two or three viable routes that differ in mechanism, inside the owner's constraints and including the current approach while it is still admissible, each with the observation that would separate it from the others. Name the assumptions that would change the route, run the smallest authorized probe or prototype for each before choosing, and bind the choice to the result; an assumption still untested keeps the commitments that depend on it provisional, and the plan says which. Open questions in the plan are answered the same way, by observation rather than by review of the abstract plan. For a plan costly to reverse, whoever settles it carries one question into its review directive: how could this plan meet its written Acceptance and still fail its purpose? The reviewer investigates concrete failure mechanisms with the evidence and methods of the field that owns the plan's dominant risk, and returns each supported mechanism as a finding and each examined one it rejects with the reason.
