# First-time-user walkthrough

Fresh observers without the producer's reasoning walk a new feature, page or flow on the real build and return problems to fix. Picks among candidates, taste and style stay with their owner.

## Observers

Each run uses as many simulated users as the change's people and risks call for, and one reviewer.

- A **simulated user** plays one person with a real goal (entry to first completed job), operates the build at the target viewport, thinks aloud, stops at the goal, five tries at one obstacle or a time limit, then rates how hard it was and says where it wanted to quit. It starts as a newcomer: online, at the real entry, signed out or newly signed up. Pair the person the change serves, from the project's persona library when it has one, with one behaviour: **impatient** (skims, taps the most prominent control, quits waits over eight seconds), **skeptic** (reads terms and data choices first), **small screen, slow network** (narrowest viewport, rotation, backgrounding mid-flow), **literal reader** (flags any term it cannot act on) or **breaker** (double taps, back mid-step, odd input, offline, restart, sign out and return).
- The **reviewer** runs the `experience-design` research checks an agent can run alone over the same screens and states.

## Findings

Each finding names its place, what it breaks, its severity and a fix: text nobody needs, a step that can go, a state a person cannot read, a moment likely to leave regret or anxiety. The owner merges duplicates, reproduces each, and labels it **product**, **harness noise** or **recheck**; rank product findings by observers hit and severity. Per goal, record completion, steps, back-tracks and the ease rating as paired diagnostics: fewer steps count only with completion held. Name what was simulated or not run. Keep findings with the delivery for the project's improvement loop to compare with later real-user reports.

Repeat while a fresh run still finds problems worth fixing.
